## The answer in plain words

The question (DESIGN §1): for each GWAS index SNP, how many SNPs this strongly BP-biased would neutral drift have produced anywhere in the genome, under the population history fitted from the genome itself?

1. **At most peaks, drift readily produces differences this large.** Seven of the eleven units are in the top tier: BAM Peaks 4 and 5, BIK Peaks 2, 3 and 6, BAVA Peak 3 and BIR Peak 3.
   - The fitted histories predict hundreds to thousands of equally differentiated SNPs in the genome: E under the best model is 123 to 15,690 (lowest bootstrap bound 108), and E_robust is 319 to 18,150.
   - The genome outside the GWAS peaks holds 347 to 11,709 of them (O).
   - For these loci, allele-frequency differentiation is not evidence for selection. It is not evidence against it either: a selected allele also ends up differentiated.
2. **Three units have no admissible model** (BAVA Peaks 2 and 6, BIR Peak 2). They use the label-free analysis, and no label-free model of BAVA or BIR passed the gates. The genome holds 955 to 2,577 SNPs at least as differentiated, so this level of differentiation is common there too.
3. **BAM Peak 1 is the one outlier.**
   - The BP allele differs by 0.99 in frequency between BAM and APA + BEN. No other SNP outside the GWAS peaks is this differentiated in that pair (O = 0). In the whole genome only 30 polarized SNPs reach a BP-derived difference of 0.9, and none reach 0.95.
   - Every admissible history predicts fewer than one such site: E = 0.04 to 0.32, depending on the model.
   - The pre-specified call is still "drift makes a few such sites" (tier 1–20), flagged model-dependent. The drift-generous rule (§6.1) takes the largest E available, and the background-selection correction supplies it: E_hetNe = 4.2.
   - That 4.2 comes almost entirely from five 1-Mb blocks elsewhere in the genome where local Ne is estimated at about 27% of average (65% of the sum; 25 blocks with f < 0.5 give 95%). Those blocks hold no such site either. The Peak 1 block itself has an average Ne (f = 0.97).
   - In terms of the number of possible routes to BP (K, §2): even at E = 4.2, drift is expected to make one such site only if about a quarter of all callable sites could each make a fish BP. Under the best-fitting history it would need about 1.4 × 10¹⁰ such sites, more than the genome has (about 6.2 × 10⁸ callable sites).
4. **The drift-exposure check keeps drift open.** In all four pairs the BP population drifted more than its TP reference since the split (τ_BP > τ_TP in 4 of 4). The pre-specified reading (§6.4) is that the drift explanation stays open. Two cautions:
   - The TP–TP controls show the same pattern: in each pair the less diverse population drifted more.
   - BAVA and BIR are not less diverse than TP2 (π 0.00254 and 0.00272, against 0.00239 for DOV and 0.00265 for DOG). Their high τ comes from long split times offset by gene flow in the best (M2) fits. High τ with strong migration does not mean low diversity.
5. **No genome-wide excess of BP-derived fixed differences.** The observed number of near-fixed BP-derived differences matches the best model's expectation: BIK 13,508 vs 12,507, BAVA 939 vs 922, BIR 795 vs 889 (BAM 0.7 vs 3.7). So there is no sign of widespread selection in the BP populations. TP-derived near-fixed differences run 1.4 to 3 times above expectation in the three southern pairs; the models do not explain this.

**What this means for the manuscript.** Differentiation cannot carry a selection argument at Peaks 2 to 6, because differences that large are common in these genomes. Peak 1 in BAM is the exception. Nothing else in the BAM genome is as differentiated, and every fitted history puts drift's expectation below one site. Even so, the pre-specified drift-generous rule leaves it at "drift makes a few", and the drift-exposure check does not argue against drift. The Summary, ¶33–36 and ¶56 still carry the earlier selection claims; they are untouched pending J. Gallant's review.

## Models and checks

- **Fits.** 60 starts per pair × model in three rounds, plus the nested-start guard (Deviation 2, Addendum 2a). No model ends below a sub-model.
- **Convergence** (adjusted rule, Deviation 2): all fits converged except A3w M3 and A4w M3 (adjusted gaps 2.29 and 1.86). The label-free M3f fits of BAVA and BIR converge only with very large linkage factors (c = 4,535 and 7,986). They push the BP founder fraction to s_BP ≈ 0.98–0.99 and BP-from-TP migration to 26 (BAVA) and 30 (BIR, at its upper bound). Both fail the adequacy gate (observed / expected 5.5 and 4.1 at Δ = 1), so neither is admissible.
- **Misidentification:** at most 2.7% in every polarized model (*szaboi*-only run: 3–10%).
- **Admissible models** (converged, misidentification ≤ 5%, adequacy gate):
  - BAM: M0, M2, M3, M0f–M3f (M1 fails the gate). BIK: M0–M3 and M3f. BAVA: M2 only. BIR: M2 only. Controls: all eight.
  - Best: BAM M2 / M3f; BIK M3 / M3f; BAVA M2; BIR M2; APA–BEN M2 / M3f; DOV–DOG M2 / M2f.
- **Adequacy at the extremes** (observed / expected at the most extreme ladder threshold with E ≥ 10, best polarized model): BAM 0.38 at Δ ≥ 0.9; BIK 1.65, BAVA 2.80 and BIR 1.65 at Δ = 1. The BAM models over-predict near-fixed differences, which is drift-generous. The BAVA model under-predicts them almost threefold, but BAVA's own units are in the top tier anyway.
- **Model against data** (`checks/model_checks.tsv`): model π, dxy and Fst lie within about 25% of the data for every admissible model. The sign of Tajima's D in the BP population matches the data in every admissible model of the four headline pairs (BIK data +1.69). In the DOV–DOG control, whose data value is near zero (−0.04 to −0.16), M0 and the four label-free models get the sign wrong.
- **Grid check** (§9.3): the corner cells of the headline pairs change by at most 2.4% between pts and 2 × pts. The control pairs' corners change by 17–83%, so every control model is flagged "numerically unreliable". Those corners are nearly empty (no control SNP reaches 0.95). E on the doubled grid joins E_robust for every unit (Addendum 2b).
- **Spectra checks** (§9.2): pass for every pair and variant.
- **Parameter recovery** (§9.3): Poisson spectra were simulated from each pair's best polarized model and, where one is admissible, its best label-free model, then refitted 20 times. All 108 quantities (every parameter, and E at every index SNP) have their true value inside the 2.5–97.5% range of the refits.
- **Bootstrap** (§5): 100 block-bootstrap refits for each pair's best models plus its M3 and M3f (18 fits). The bootstrap and recovery array (400 tasks) completed without failures.

## Caveats

1. **BAM Peak 1's tier rests on E_hetNe.** Under every admissible model the point E is below 1 (0.04–0.32). Only the background-selection correction lifts E_robust above 1. That correction assumes the low-π/divergence ratio of a few blocks reflects low local Ne, and those blocks contain no qualifying SNP.
2. **Split time trades against migration.** For BAM, M0 (no migration) puts the split at T = 0.046 × 2N_anc generations and M2 at 3.4. Both are admissible and both fit the tail. The §10 sentence uses the best model, so the clause about new mutations is model-dependent. Peak 1's E differs by sevenfold between them (0.32 vs 0.046).
3. **Models that did not converge or sit at bounds.** BAVA and BIR M3 did not converge, and their M3f fits fail the adequacy gate; none of them is admissible. In the APA–BEN control, BP-from-TP migration sits at its upper bound (30) in M2, M2f, M3 and M3f. These stay admissible, but they are control models, already flagged numerically unreliable, and enter no headline call.
4. **Controls are numerically unreliable** at the corners (see the grid check). They still show the expected pattern: no near-fixed differences, and observed / expected 1.2 (APA–BEN, Δ ≥ 0.6) and 1.9 (DOV–DOG, Δ ≥ 0.7) at their gate thresholds.
5. **Sensitivity rows not run:** §7 rows 1 (GWAS wobble-excluded lists), 2 (single TP references), 3 (BIK + BAVA pooled), 4 (exons ±5 kb), 6 (5-Mb bootstrap blocks), 7 (singletons masked), 8 (paralog filter off) and 9 (LD proxy for Peaks 4 and 5). Row 5 (label-free) is part of the headline. By §7, no sensitivity row can change a headline tier except through §6.3; why the single-TP override cannot change a call is in DESIGN "Notes made after the results", note 1.
6. **E counts sites, not independent events.** Linked sites drift together, so E overstates the number of independent drift outcomes. The Markov bound (P(at least one) ≤ E) holds whatever the linkage, so E is conservative in the drift-generous direction.
